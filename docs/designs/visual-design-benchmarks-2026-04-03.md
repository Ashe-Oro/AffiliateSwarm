Sites analyzed: PartnerStack, impact.com, Awin (ShareASale), CJ, Rakuten Advertising, GRIN, Rewardful

## Individual Site Analyses

### 1. PartnerStack (partnerstack.com) -- B2B Partner Platform

**Color palette:** Deep purple-blue (#434DE1) as signature brand color on white backgrounds. Dark charcoal (#747474) for secondary text. Clean, limited palette.

**Hero:** Two-column asymmetrical layout, left-aligned text with right visual (animated globe showing real-time partner data). Curved SVG footer element transitions hero to next section. Heading scales from 5rem to 5.25vw responsively.

**Typography:** Inter (300-700 weights) as primary sans-serif. Newsreader loaded as secondary. Generous letter-spacing on headings, clear weight differentiation between hierarchy levels.

**CTAs:** Rounded 8px corners, #434DE1 purple fill with white text, 600 font-weight. Input-button combos for email capture. Purple text arrows for secondary actions.

**Graphics:** Animated globe visualization, SVG map cards with colored left-band accents, continuous-scroll logo marquee (680s animation loop).

**Social proof:** G2 badge (4.7 stars, 820 reviews), "600+ companies" stat, rotating testimonial carousel (8.5s interval), named customers (monday.com), "115,000+ active partners."

**Overall feel:** Modern, professional, approachable. Confident without being aggressive. White space dominant.

## Cross-Platform Pattern Synthesis

### 7 Key Design Principles Shared by the Best Sites

**1. Single Signature Color + Strategic Accent**

Every strong site commits to ONE dominant brand color and pairs it with a single contrasting accent. PartnerStack owns purple. CJ owns teal. GRIN owns purple+lime. impact.com is the exception with a multi-color gradient, but even that functions as one unified "gradient identity." Sites that try to use too many colors feel generic. AffiliateSwarm needs one ownable signature color.

**2. Dark Mode = Premium + Tech-Forward**

GRIN's dark-mode hero is the most visually distinctive and modern design in the entire set. Dark backgrounds with colored accent glows signal innovation, AI, and technical sophistication. The enterprise incumbents (CJ, Rakuten, Awin) all use white backgrounds and feel dated by comparison. For an AI-agent-focused network, dark mode is a strong category differentiator.

**3. Stats as Visual Architecture, Not Decoration**

The most credible sites (CJ, impact.com, Rakuten) make numerical stats into visual landmarks -- large type, prominent placement, acting as section anchors. "1B+ Monthly" at CJ is practically a design element. Stats function as both social proof AND visual rhythm.

**4. Continuous Logo Marquees Create Ambient Trust**

Every site uses auto-scrolling logo carousels. The best implementations (PartnerStack at 680s, Rewardful at 44s) run continuously and feel ambient rather than aggressive. This is table stakes for the category.

**5. Pill-Shaped CTAs Signal Modernity**

The most modern-feeling sites (Rakuten, GRIN, PartnerStack) use highly-rounded or pill-shaped buttons (border-radius: 9999px or 30px). Square-cornered buttons read as 2020-era. Full pill shapes with generous padding feel current.

**6. Typography Weight as Primary Hierarchy Tool**

The best sites use a single font family across many weights rather than multiple fonts. TTCommons (CJ), Roobert (GRIN), Inter (PartnerStack) -- each deployed in 5-7 weights. Heading-to-body contrast comes from weight and size, not font-family switching. Custom/distinctive fonts (Roobert, TTCommons) feel more premium than system/Google fonts.

**7. Animated Hero Elements Create Perceived Sophistication**

PartnerStack's globe, GRIN's floating dots and video, impact.com's gradient cycling -- the top sites all have subtle motion in the hero that creates a "living" feeling without being distracting. Static heroes feel like templates.

## Design Brief: Recommendations for AffiliateSwarm

### Brand Color System

**Primary recommendation: Deep indigo-violet with electric teal accent**

Rationale: Purple/violet is heavily claimed in this space (PartnerStack, GRIN, Rakuten). Rather than competing, AffiliateSwarm should own the space between -- a deep indigo that reads as distinct from both PartnerStack's blue-purple and GRIN's bright violet. Pair with an electric teal/cyan accent for CTAs and highlights.

Suggested palette:

- **Primary:** Deep indigo `#2D1B69` to `#3B1F8E` range (darker, more mysterious than competitors)
- **Accent:** Electric teal `#00E5C3` or seafoam `#4AEDC4` (maritime connection, distinctive from CJ's warmer teal)
- **Surface dark:** Near-black with blue undertone `#0A0B14` to `#12141F`
- **Surface light:** Warm off-white `#F8F7F4` (avoids sterile pure white)
- **Text primary:** `#E8E6F0` on dark, `#1A1625` on light
- **Warning/energy:** Warm amber `#FFB547` for stats and highlights

The teal accent creates a natural "maritime" association with "AffiliateSwarm" while feeling technical and fresh. The indigo is ownable -- no competitor in this space uses it.

### Hero Section

**Pattern: Dark-mode hero with ambient motion + dual-path CTA**

Drawing from GRIN's approach but adapted for AffiliateSwarm's agent + human positioning:

- **Dark background** (`#0A0B14`) with subtle radial gradient glow in indigo emanating from center
- **Animated particles or connection lines** suggesting agent-to-agent network activity (like PartnerStack's globe concept but more abstract -- floating nodes with thin connecting lines that pulse with teal light)
- **Dual CTA cards** below the headline: one for Affiliates (agent-first), one for Advertisers -- similar to GRIN's split-path design but using card elevation and the teal accent border
- **Headline treatment:** Large (56-72px), semi-bold, tracking-tight. Two-line with the second line in the teal accent color
- **Subhead:** 18-20px, 400 weight, muted text color, max-width 640px centered

### Typography

**Recommendation: Single premium sans-serif system**

- **Primary font:** A geometric sans-serif like Satoshi, General Sans, or Switzer (all free, high-quality, distinctive). Alternatively, Inter if budget/simplicity is preferred, but a distinctive typeface creates instant premium perception.
- **Weight range:** 400 (body), 500 (subheads/UI), 600 (section heads), 700 (hero/primary heads)
- **Heading sizes:** H1: 56-72px / H2: 36-44px / H3: 24-28px / Body: 16-18px
- **Line heights:** Headings 1.1-1.2, body 1.6
- **Letter spacing:** -0.02em on headings (tight), normal on body
- **No serif fonts.** Every successful platform in this space is pure sans-serif. Serifs read as editorial/publishing, not as technical infrastructure.

### CTA Buttons

**Primary CTA:**
- Teal accent fill (`#00E5C3` or similar)
- Dark text (`#0A0B14`)
- Pill shape (border-radius: 9999px)
- Padding: 14px 32px
- Font: 15px, weight 500
- Hover: lighter teal + subtle glow shadow (`box-shadow: 0 4px 20px rgba(0, 229, 195, 0.3)`)
- Active: scale(0.97)

**Secondary CTA:**
- Transparent fill with 1px teal border
- Teal text
- Same pill shape
- Hover: teal fill at 10% opacity

**Ghost/tertiary:**
- Text link with right arrow, teal color
- Hover: underline or slight translate-x

### Gradients and Visual Effects

- **Hero gradient:** Radial gradient from center -- indigo core fading to near-black edges. Subtle, atmospheric, not flashy.
- **Section transitions:** Use subtle gradient bands (indigo to transparent) at section boundaries instead of hard border-top lines.
- **Card backgrounds:** Slight glass-morphism on dark mode -- `background: rgba(255,255,255,0.03)` with `backdrop-filter: blur(20px)` and thin `border: 1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.06)`.
- **Accent glows:** Teal glow effects behind key stats or feature icons (radial gradient, very soft).
- **No multi-color gradients** (impact.com's rainbow approach feels busy and is hard to maintain consistently).

### Section Patterns

**Section 1 -- Hero:** Dark, animated, centered headline, dual CTA cards.

**Section 2 -- Stats bar:** Full-width dark section with 3-4 large teal numbers (commission volume, active agents, campaigns, etc.). Numbers in 48-56px, labels in 14px uppercase tracking-wide.

**Section 3 -- How it works:** Light background (`#F8F7F4`). Three-column with numbered steps, simple line icons in teal, short descriptions. Clean and scannable.

**Section 4 -- Role split:** Two large cards side-by-side. Dark card for affiliates, light card with dark border for advertisers. Each with icon, 3-4 bullet points, CTA button.

**Section 5 -- Social proof:** Logo marquee on dark background. Continuous scroll. Grayscale logos that brighten on hover.

**Section 6 -- Testimonials/Case studies:** Cards on light background with pull-quote, attribution photo, company name, and a key metric highlighted in teal.

**Section 7 -- Final CTA:** Dark section with centered headline, single primary CTA, subtle particle animation callback to hero.

### Social Proof Strategy

Minimum viable social proof for credibility in this category:

1. Logo marquee (continuous scroll, minimum 8-12 logos)
2. One prominent stat (total commissions processed, active agents, campaigns)
3. At least 2-3 named testimonials with photos
4. Trust badge (if available -- G2, ProductHunt, etc.)

### What NOT to Do (Anti-Patterns from Weaker Sites)

- **Avoid pure white backgrounds everywhere** (Awin/ShareASale feels dated)
- **Avoid stock photography** (Rakuten's lifestyle photos feel generic)
- **Avoid multiple font families** (Rewardful loads Ubuntu + Montserrat + Rubik -- unfocused)
- **Avoid static heroes with no motion** (reads as template)
- **Avoid warm/friendly color palettes** (greens, oranges feel like SMB tools, not infrastructure)
- **Avoid traditional "Request a Demo" as primary CTA** -- self-serve model is a differentiator; lean into "Start now" energy

### Competitive Positioning Through Design

The affiliate platform design landscape splits into two camps:

1. **Enterprise incumbents** (CJ, Rakuten, Awin): White backgrounds, corporate photography, trust-through-legacy positioning. Clean but forgettable.

2. **Modern challengers** (GRIN, PartnerStack, impact.com): Dark modes, animated heroes, gradient accents, AI messaging. Visually ambitious and memorable.

AffiliateSwarm should firmly position in camp 2 but push further -- leaning into the dark-mode, agent-first, technically sophisticated aesthetic. The "agent economy" positioning is unique in this space; the design should feel like it belongs to a NEW category, not like a variation on existing affiliate platforms.

The closest visual reference point is GRIN's dark-mode approach, but adapted with AffiliateSwarm's indigo+teal palette and more abstract/technical graphics (network visualizations, connection nodes) instead of GRIN's creator-focused imagery.
